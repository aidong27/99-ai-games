METHOD v1

Transparent engineering observation

What this benchmark measures, and what it does not

Protocol 99 compares an AI coding system: the model, its Agent product, its native tools, and the permissions available during one recorded Run. It is not a pure foundation-model experiment and it does not claim scientific model IQ.

Comparison unit Raw and Repair Browser evidence Compliance score Identity Blind comparison Legacy boundary Limitations

01 · Unit

Model + Agent + native tool environment

Codex, Claude Code, Grok Build, Kimi, GLM coding tools, and similar products expose different shells, browsers, context systems, and permissions. Results therefore describe the complete coding system actually used.

02 · Run protocol

Raw history cannot be polished away

A Raw Run begins from an assigned empty workspace and one canonical prompt. The Agent may test and repair its own work before declaring completion. Finalization records a stable source hash. A later Standard Repair copies the Run into a new directory and preserves the Raw source unchanged.

03 · Verification

Victory must happen through public controls

Chromium executes participant tests through a restricted SDK. Tests may press keys, click visible controls, wait, take checkpoints, and read window.__P99__.getState(). They may not mutate state, teleport, inject victory, disable hazards, or call hidden internals.

04 · Automated Compliance

A traceable 100-point rules score

Every point maps to a public boolean check for startup, required states, the core-relay-exit objective, world-stage changes, hazards, ability limitation, deterministic playthroughs, security, integrity, accessibility, and mobile layout. Subjective Player Experience and Engineering Reviews stay separate and show “not reviewed” when absent.

05 · Provenance

Unknown stays unknown

Provider, model, version, Agent, and Agent version are recorded from explicit declarations or reliable tool facts. The repository never infers a precise version from branding, dates, or style. Every identity includes a source and confidence label.

06 · Bias control

Blind Compare is local, not fake social proof

When at least two verified same-version Entries exist, the browser can hide names, present both playable versions, collect one local preference, and then reveal identity and evidence. There is no backend and no fabricated global vote count.

07 · Historical boundary

Eleven creative experiments stay outside the ranking

The Pre-Benchmark Era used different prompts, genres, revision paths, and constraints. Those games remain playable and their provenance remains intact, but their count and Hall coverage are reported separately from Protocol 99.

08 · Limits

Transparent, not falsely absolute

A public repository can express a no-peeking protocol but cannot prove a perfect blind sandbox. Browser automation verifies observable behavior, not every design virtue. One benchmark cannot represent all software engineering. Results are engineering observations bounded to one prompt version and one recorded environment.

Cross-version results are never silently mixed into one ranking.
